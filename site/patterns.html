Patterns

Four ways of reading the same award records. Each one is a count, set beside something to compare it with.

None of these is a finding against an agency. Many ordinary reasons produce the same numbers, and the records leave out how many bidders took part and which procurement method was used.

Bunching

How many awards land just under an amount

For each amount, count the awards up to 10% below it and the awards up to 10% above it, then divide. Grey bars are round amounts with no rule attached. They show what round numbers do by themselves.

Loading the records…

₱5,000,000 bunches as strongly as the ceiling, and no rule on file explains it. A newspaper advertising requirement once applied at that amount, but it ended in October 2018 and the bunching grew after 2021. It is kept out of the no-rule baseline.

Rule change

The ceiling moved to ₱2,000,000 in February 2025

If agencies steer contracts under a ceiling, bunching should fade at ₱1,000,000 and grow at ₱2,000,000 once the new rules took effect. So far it has done neither clearly.

2025 is a partial year, and most 2025 records arrived through a different export. Months with fewer than 20 awards just over ₱2,000,000 are left off the chart.

Same day

Several notices, one supplier, one day

A group is three or more separate notices from one agency to one supplier on one day, each within the ceiling in force, adding up to more than it.

Only records with a reference number are counted, since without one a notice cannot be told apart from a lot. That removes most of 2025. No ceiling is on file before late 2016.
